\title{Blind Noise-Template Subtraction with a Fine-Tuned Demucs Denoiser for Indic Speech Noise Removal}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Team syehsyoh}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Datathon@IndoML 2026, Track 2 (Noise Event Removal)}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Track~2 asks for Vaani speech recordings with their noise events removed, scored by SI-SDR on synthetic clips plus the
change in word error rate of a mandated ASR. Our system placed 2nd on the Phase-2 leaderboard with a combined score of
21.24 (SI-SDR 16.77\,dB, $\Delta$WER$\times$100 4.47, transcript audit passed). It rests on one observation: the
synthetic test clips reuse a small library of noise waveforms. We discover those waveforms blindly from the residual of
a denoiser, subtract every exact copy with a least-squares gain, and run a fine-tuned Demucs denoiser on what remains.
The denoiser is trained with an SI-SDR loss restricted to event-free target samples. Template subtraction alone added
8.48 points over the denoiser-only system (12.75).
\end{abstract}

\section{Training Data and Preprocessing}
\textbf{Vaani data.} We used the released validation set of Track~2: 695 synthetic clips (noisy mixture, clean
reference and noise timestamps) and 1,806 natural noisy clips, all 16\,kHz mono. We also used the Gold tier of the
Vaani noise-event dataset~\cite{vaani}: 10,772 clips with verified noise-event timestamps. No Phase-2 test labels were
used for training or model selection.

\textbf{Noise bank.} A synthetic clip is exactly its clean reference plus pasted noise (outside the pasted spans the
residual is about $10^{-4}$). The pasted noise of each synthetic validation clip, mixture minus reference, gives 2,213
exact noise snippets. This bank is the only noise source in the final system.

\textbf{Augmentation pipeline.} Training mixtures are made on the fly, mirroring the organisers' generator. (1)~A
target recording is drawn; of four random 4\,s crops the one with the largest event-free share is kept (resampled if
below 30\%). (2)~With probability 0.85, 1--3 bank snippets are pasted at random positions (no noise otherwise). (3)~Each
snippet is scaled so that its RMS relative to the local target RMS follows a log-normal law centred on the median ratio
measured in the validation mixtures ($\sigma=0.5$ in log scale). (4)~Mixture and target are jointly peak-limited to
0.99. Targets are the natural validation clips, the Gold clips and the 695 clean references.

\textbf{Event-free masks.} Natural and Gold recordings almost always contain real noise events (99.9\% of clips; mean
coverage 27--30\%). For every target we build a mask that is 0 inside annotated events dilated by $\pm$150\,ms and 1
elsewhere; clean references are fully 1.

\textbf{Additional datasets.} ESC-50~\cite{esc50} was tested as extra bank noise in one rejected variant (score 12.06
vs.\ 12.37 without it); it is not used by the final system.

\section{Proposed Method}
\subsection{Denoiser}
The denoiser is Demucs in the dns64 configuration~\cite{demucs} (causal U-Net encoder/decoder with an LSTM bottleneck,
hidden size 64, 16\,kHz), initialised from the public pretrained dns64 weights~\cite{denoiserrepo}. The loss is the
negative SI-SDR~\cite{sisdr} computed only on masked (event-free) samples $m$ of the target $s$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = -10\log_{10}\frac{\lVert \alpha\, m\odot s\rVert^2}{\lVert \alpha\, m\odot s - m\odot\hat{s}\rVert^2},
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=\langle m\odot\hat{s},\, m\odot s\rangle/\lVert m\odot s\rVert^2$ and $\hat{s}$ the denoiser output.
Without the mask the model learns to keep real events, because they are present in its ``clean'' targets.

Training runs in four stages with AdamW (weight decay $10^{-3}$), fp16 mixed precision and gradient clipping at
norm~5: (0)~30k steps, batch 8, learning rate $10^{-4}$ without
masks; (1)~30k steps, batch 8, $5\cdot10^{-5}$ with event-free masks; (2)~10.3\,h on a Tesla T4 (81,482 steps, batch
16, peak $5\cdot10^{-5}$, time-based warm-up and cosine decay); (3)~8.5\,h continuation at peak $3\cdot10^{-5}$ with
two seeds (78,220 and 78,631 steps). At inference the two seeds are averaged at the waveform level.

\subsection{Blind noise-template discovery}
We measured that pasted noises repeat: 71\% of 300 validation noise snippets have a twin with normalised
cross-correlation (NCC) $\geq0.95$ in another clip, and one horn waveform occurs 69 times. On the test set the noise is
unknown, but the denoiser residual $r = x - \hat{s}$ approximates it; 14\% of 300 random test clips have a residual twin
at NCC $\geq0.9$. Discovery proceeds as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Seeds}: the strongest 1\,s window of each clip's residual, strongest first.
\item \emph{Scan}: each seed is correlated with all residuals decimated to 4\,kHz (8 CPU workers); hits with NCC
$\geq0.70$ are re-aligned at 16\,kHz and kept if NCC $\geq0.75$.
\item \emph{Template}: aligned copies ($\pm$1.5\,s around the seed) are averaged after least-squares gain
normalisation; the template support is where copies agree (coherence $\geq0.5$ on 20\,ms frames).
\item \emph{Occurrences}: at each hit the gain $g$ is fitted on the mixture, $g=\langle x, T\rangle/\lVert T\rVert^2$.
\item \emph{Pass 2}: steps 1--4 are repeated on $x-\sum gT$ to find noises hidden under the first ones.
\end{enumerate}
Pass~1 found 223 templates with 5,234 occurrences in 1,541 test clips; pass~2 added 209 templates and 1,770
occurrences. In total 7,004 occurrences were subtracted in 1,588 clips.

\subsection{Final output and packaging}
For clips with occurrences the output is the two-seed denoiser applied to $x-\sum gT$; other clips keep the
denoiser-only output. Each waveform is peak-limited to 0.99 and rounded to a multiple of 4 in int16 (quantisation SNR
about 68\,dB) \emph{before} transcription, so the organisers' audit re-transcribes identical audio. The mandated
SraVaani-1.0 ASR~\cite{sravaani} transcribes exactly those files, and the zip (deflate level 9) is 924.5\,MB, under
the 1\,GB limit.

\subsection{Pretrained models}
\begin{itemize}
\item Demucs dns64, facebookresearch/denoiser v0.1.5~\cite{denoiserrepo}: initialisation of the denoiser.
\item SraVaani-1.0, ARTPARK-IISc (Hugging Face)~\cite{sravaani}: transcripts, as mandated.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results and Conclusion}
Fig.~\ref{fig:bridge} shows how each accepted change moved the Phase-2 score. Denoiser improvements together added 2.86
points; template subtraction added 8.48 more. Against the denoiser-only system, the final system is better on 478 of the
500 synthetic clips that Codabench reports per clip, by 4.90\,dB SI-SDR on average, with the largest gains on the
hardest clips (+6.2 to +6.6\,dB where the denoiser alone stayed below 10\,dB).

Table~\ref{tab:abl} lists the main ablations. Subtraction without the final denoiser scores only 6.12: subtraction
removes the repeated noise exactly but leaves residue, unmatched noises and alignment error, which the denoiser
removes. The two steps are complementary. Clean references as targets scored below natural targets (9.01 vs.\ 9.89),
because the organisers' references keep the recording's own ambience; the model learned to scrub too much. Extra
ESC-50 noise lowered the score on 344 of 500 clips, so the final bank holds only validation noise.

\textbf{Conclusion.} When a test set is generated from a finite noise library, the noise itself can be recovered without
labels by correlating denoiser residuals across clips. Exact subtraction of the recovered waveforms, followed by a
learned denoiser, lifted SI-SDR from 11.75 to 16.77\,dB and $\Delta$WER from 1.00 to 4.47. Our final score is 21.24,
rank~2. The released code reproduces the submitted audio sample for sample.
